# Search your problem in your own words and count the sellers

*The words you use for your problem are not the words the industry uses to sell the fix.*

Here is something you can test in about a minute. It explains a frustration a lot of business owners have quietly decided is their own fault, and it is not.

## The two searches

Search for your problem the way you would say it out loud to another person.

> "My phone stopped ringing."
>
> "Which platform should I use for my shop."
>
> "Nobody can find us when they search for our trade."

Now search for the same problem the way an advertisement would put it.

> "Lead generation."
>
> "Conversion rate optimisation."
>
> "Local SEO services."

Same problem, both times. Two completely different sets of people come back.

## What each one returns

The second search returns an industry. Agencies, consultants, tools, courses. Every result is somebody who sells the fix, because those phrases are worth money and have been bid on for twenty years.

The first search returns other owners. Forum threads, a few honest answers, a lot of silence. Almost nobody is advertising against the sentence a worried person actually types at eleven o'clock at night, because that sentence does not look commercial to the people buying keywords.

## Why this is worth a minute of your time

When you search in your own words and find nothing useful, you reach one of three conclusions. There is no help for this. It is not a real problem. Everyone else has it figured out and you are the only one who does not.

All three are wrong. What happened is a vocabulary mismatch. The help exists — it is filed under a phrase you would never say, by people who have never once heard a customer say it either.

I notice this because I sit on the other side of it. Looking for owners describing real problems in their own words is genuinely hard, and searching in industry vocabulary finds nothing but other people in the industry, talking to each other, in a language none of their customers use.

## The direction that costs you money

Here is the part worth sitting with, because it runs the other way as well.

Your website is written in your words. The words you use with customers, about the work you actually do. That is correct and you should not stop.

But the systems now deciding whether to mention your business — a search engine, and increasingly an assistant answering a question in a sentence — are matching your page against how a *customer* asks. Not how an agency writes, and not how you would describe your craft to another tradesman.

A plumber's site that says "hydronic systems and backflow certification" is accurate. Someone typing "my radiator is cold and I don't know who to call" may never reach it. Neither party is wrong. Nothing joined them up.

That gap is not a marketing problem in the sense of needing a campaign. It is closer to a translation problem, and the fix is usually to say, somewhere on the page, the thing a customer would actually say — in their words, next to yours.

## Do the test

Run the two searches. Not to buy anything, and not because it leads anywhere in particular.

Run them so you can see with your own eyes that the gap is real, that it is structural rather than personal, and that it was never your reading comprehension.

Then have a look at your own page and ask the harder version of the question: if a customer described this problem in the way they would actually describe it, is there anything on my site that matches?
